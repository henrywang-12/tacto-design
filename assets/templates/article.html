Why drawing parts have no price basis, and what a should-cost model changes

Northdesk research12 November 20267 min read

Should-cost Part 4471-BAlSi10Mg

A standard part has a market price. A drawing part has only the price someone once agreed to. That is where much of a manufacturer's savings potential hides.

Screws, bearings and cables are listed in catalogues. A buyer can compare them in minutes.

A machined bracket made to your own drawing exists nowhere else. There is no list price, no index, and often only one supplier who has ever made it.

A price without a basis

In most companies the price of a drawing part was set years ago, in the first RFQ. Since then it has moved in one direction.

Every increase arrives with a reason: aluminium, energy, wages. Few buyers can check whether the reason holds for this part, because nobody knows what the part is made of in euros.

So the old price becomes the basis for the new one. Across thousands of part numbers, that adds up.

What a part is really made of

A should-cost model takes the part apart on paper. It reads the drawing and estimates what each step costs at a fair supplier.

Material follows weight and alloy, priced with the day's index. Machining time follows the features: bores, threads, tolerances, surfaces. Set-up is spread across the lot size.

None of this needs the supplier's books. It needs the drawing, the market, and enough past quotes to calibrate.

“For the first time we know what a part should cost before the supplier tells us. That changed every conversation we have.”Jana Petrovic, Head of purchasing, Auerberg Guss

What changes for buyers

  • Negotiations start with a number and a reason, not with last year's price.
  • Price increases are checked against the indices that drive this part.
  • RFQs go to suppliers whose machines fit the part, not only to the one who made it before.
  • The largest gaps come first, so time goes where the money is.

The supplier quoted €18.40 for part 4471-B. The model puts it at €15.50. The gap of €2.90 sits in machining time. At 12,000 parts a year, that is €34,800.

Prepare the negotiation

Where to start

Not with every part at once. Start with the part numbers that carry the most volume and have not been put out to tender in three years.

In most plants that is a few hundred parts. Together they usually hold more than half of the savings potential.

The models improve with every quote that comes back. After a year, buyers know the price basis of every drawing part they buy.
