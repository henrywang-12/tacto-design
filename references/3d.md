# 3D parts

Whenever a screen or a page shows a part or an assembly in 3D, it looks the same: a dark metal part under soft studio light, no outlines, a faint shadow on the floor. It turns slowly on its own, you can drag it and throw it, and an assembly can be pulled apart into an exploded view. This follows how a part is shown in Tacto's product (the Geometry card of an article: a grey model you rotate to inspect).

The look lives in `assets/tacto-3d.js`. You describe what to show; you never write three.js, materials or lights yourself.

Contents: Use it · The look · Parts that ship · Your own part · Your own assembly · Moving it · Status colours · Where it belongs · Mistakes

## Use it

```html
<div class="tc-part" data-tc-part="housing"></div>

<script src="vendor/three.min.js"></script>
<script src="tacto-3d.js"></script>
```

`scripts/inline.py` bundles both scripts into the file (three.js r128, MIT, ships in `assets/vendor/`). Give the box a height: `style="--tc-part-h: 24rem"`, or put it in `.tc-app__viewer` (320px, `--tall` 440px).

In the product screen, wrap it in the viewer card:

```html
<div class="tc-app__viewer tc-app__viewer--tall">
  <div class="tc-part" id="asm" data-tc-part="station" data-grid></div>
  <div class="tc-app__viewer-title">Folding station<small>148 drawing parts</small></div>
  <div class="tc-app__viewer-bar">
    <div class="tc-app__keys"> ... status keys, see below ... </div>
    <button type="button" class="tc-app__btn tc-app__btn--outline tc-app__btn--sm" data-tc-explode="#asm" aria-pressed="false"><i data-tc-icon="boxes"></i>Exploded view</button>
  </div>
</div>
```

Template: `assets/templates/dashboard-article.html` (one part, and an assembly). On a web page: `assets/templates/scroll-story.html`.

## The look

Fixed in the script, the same for every part. Do not override it per page.

There are two looks. The script picks one; `data-look` forces the other.

| | **Metal** (the default) | **Drawing** |
|---|---|---|
| When | a single part; an assembly without status colours; anything on a web page | an assembly that shows status colours (picked automatically), or `data-look="drawing"` |
| Where it comes from | the Geometry card in Tacto's product: a dark part you rotate to inspect | the engineering-drawing look: pale solids with every edge drawn |
| Surface | gunmetal `#4B4F57`, glossy (roughness 0.3, metalness 0.78), reflecting a soft studio | pale grey `#CFD3D8`, matt, or the status colour |
| Edges | none: the form is read from light and reflection | thin lines, `#3F3F46` at about half strength |
| On black (`data-theme="dark"`) | brighter steel `#A9AFB8`, a little ember reflected from behind, no floor shadow | same pale parts, no floor shadow |

Common to both: sky light from above with one sun and one weak fill; a soft round shadow on the floor (`data-grid` adds a pale grid for an assembly); no background of its own, so the white card or the panel behind it shows through; the selected part glows slightly. No textures, no chrome mirror, no glass, no coloured parts outside the status colours.

## Parts that ship

Single parts: `bar` · `plate` · `block` · `angle` · `gusset` · `sleeve` · `shaft` · `flange` · `housing` · `frame`. Assemblies: `unit` (a bearing unit, 18 parts, comes apart along its shaft) and `station` (a machine station, 148 drawing parts with status colours). In the browser console: `TcParts.names()`.

Pick the one closest to what the screen is about. A sheet-metal supplier gets `angle` or `plate`, a turned part `housing` or `flange`, a machine builder `station`.

## Your own part

Describe it as a list of solids, in millimetres (x right, y up, z towards the viewer), as JSON in the attribute:

```html
<div class="tc-part" data-tc-part='[["b",120,12,60,0,6,0],["c",10,40,0,32,0,"y"],["t",24,12,30,40,27,0,"y"]]'></div>
```

| Solid | Form |
|---|---|
| box | `["b", width, height, depth, x, y, z, rotX, rotY, rotZ]`, centre at x y z, rotations in radians (0.785 is 45°) |
| cylinder | `["c", radius, length, x, y, z, axis]`, axis `"z"` (default), `"x"` or `"y"` |
| tube or drilled disc | `["t", R, r, length, x, y, z, axis, holes]`, holes `[[x, y, r], ...]` in its face |
| angle profile | `["l", legA, legB, thickness, depth, x, y, z]` |
| any outline | `["p", [[x, y], ...], depth, x, y, z, holes, axis]`, extruded along z, or along `"x"` or `"y"` for a standing plate or rib. Holes are in the outline's own coordinates. |

A worked example, a motor mount (base plate, a standing face plate with a bore and four holes, two ribs):

```json
[["b",120,10,90,0,5,0],
 ["p",[[0,0],[110,0],[110,90],[0,90]],8,-55,10,-41,[[55,48,22],[20,18,4],[90,18,4],[20,78,4],[90,78,4]]],
 ["p",[[0,0],[60,0],[0,60]],6,-52,10,-37,[],"x"],
 ["p",[[0,0],[60,0],[0,60]],6,46,10,-37,[],"x"]]
```

Build a part from a few solids that say what it is: a plate with its holes, a housing as two or three stepped tubes. Five to ten solids are plenty. Real geometry (STEP, STL) is not loaded: this is for prototypes and pictures, and the page should say "model simplified" where that matters.

## Your own assembly

A list of parts, each with a name, an optional status and its solids. `o` is where the part goes in the exploded view (an offset in mm); leave it out and the part moves away from the middle and a little up.

```html
<div class="tc-part" id="pump" data-tc-part='[
  {"n":"Base","g":"same","s":[["b",200,12,120,0,6,0]],"o":[0,-50,0]},
  {"n":"Housing","g":"new","s":[["t",40,28,80,0,60,0]]},
  {"n":"Cover","g":"near","s":[["t",52,12,8,0,60,46]],"o":[0,0,120]}
]'></div>
```

For a row of parts on one axis (a shaft with what sits on it), give every part an `o` along that axis in the order it comes off: that is the classic exploded drawing. Larger sets: `TcParts.add("pump", function () { return [...]; })` in a script, then `data-tc-part="pump"`.

## Moving it

| What | How |
|---|---|
| Turns slowly on its own | default. `data-spin="false"` to keep it still. Off with reduced motion. |
| Drag, throw | always. Letting go keeps the speed of the hand, which fades into the slow turn. The page still scrolls over it. |
| Exploded view, by button | `<button data-tc-explode="#id" aria-pressed="false">`. Eases apart and back. |
| Exploded view, fixed | `data-explode="0.6"` (0 closed, 1 fully apart) |
| Comes apart with the page | `data-explode="scroll"` inside a scroll scene (`references/motion.md`). `data-explode-range="0.15,0.7"`: the part of the scene over which it opens. `data-turn="-0.5"`: radians it turns across the scene. Turn it towards the side of the axis it opens along, never onto the axis: seen end-on, an exploded row hides itself. |
| Point at a part | assembly parts with a name show it in a small tip |
| Click a part | the element fires `tc:pick` with `detail: { index, name, status }`. Show what you know about that part next to the model. |
| From script | `el.tcPart.explode(0..1 or true/false)`, `.spin(bool)`, `.filter("new" or null)`, `.pick(i or null)` |

Parts of an assembly arrive one after another when the model first appears. That is the only entrance effect.

## Status colours

In an assembly a part can carry a status, and the colour says what is known about it. With status colours the model switches to the drawing look, because colour needs a pale ground. These are the product screen's own colours, so use them inside `.tc-app` only; on a web page set `data-status="false"` and the assembly is metal.

| Status `g` | Colour | Means |
|---|---|---|
| `same` | grey | carried over, nothing to decide |
| `exists` | green | exists already under another number |
| `near` | amber | nearly the same as an existing part |
| `new` | blue | new |
| `format` | white | set aside, not checked |

Give the model its keys, with counts, as filter buttons:

```html
<div class="tc-app__keys">
  <button type="button" data-tc-filter="new" data-tc-target="#asm"><i class="tc-part__key tc-part__key--new"></i>New 68</button>
</div>
```

A key dims every other group. Counts must add up to the number of parts named in the title. Rename the statuses in the text to fit your case; keep the colours and their order.

## Where it belongs

- **Product screen:** the Geometry card of an article, a comparison of two parts, the assembly of a BOM or a check. One model per card, at most three on a screen.
- **Web page or prototype opening:** one object as the picture of the page, on black or on the grey page. With a scroll scene it can come apart as the story is told.
- **Not** in documents, decks, emails or covers: put a screenshot there (`scripts/shoot.sh`).
- **Not** as decoration. If the page is not about a physical part, there is no model on it.

Without WebGL the box shows a plain front-view line drawing of the same solids, so the page still makes sense.

## Mistakes

- Writing three.js by hand, or changing colours, materials or lights for one page. Every part must look like every other part.
- Mirror chrome, coloured plastics, textures, bloom, depth blur, a starfield. The metal is dark and soft, not a mirror.
- A model that spins fast, bounces, or zooms on scroll wheel. It turns slowly; the wheel scrolls the page.
- An exploded view seen along its own axis.
- Status colours on a black marketing page.
- More parts than the story needs. A unit of 15 to 20 parts explodes well; 150 parts is for the product screen, where they are data.
- Calling a simplified model the real geometry.
