# Voice

How Tacto writes, read from its home page, product page, mission page, success stories and blog (about 6,500 words, Nov 2026). The look is half of the likeness; this is the other half. Styling that is right around copy that sounds like a generic software advert still reads as fake.

Contents: Who is speaking · Eight habits · Words · Punctuation and casing · Numbers · Patterns by element · Patterns by format · German · Before and after

All examples below are written for this file. They show the pattern, they are not Tacto's sentences. Do not copy sentences from tacto.ai into a deliverable.

## Who is speaking

An industrial company that happens to build AI. It speaks to heads of purchasing and CPOs of manufacturers as one engineer to another: sure of itself, short, proud of industry, and always with a customer and a number at hand. It never sounds like a start-up that is excited about itself.

## Eight habits

1. **Short declaratives, in a row.** A headline is two or three very short sentences, each with a full stop: a claim, then what follows from it. "You set the targets. Agents do the legwork." The first sentence is set in black, the rest dimmed.
2. **The buyer is the hero, the AI is the hand.** The person decides, sets strategy, keeps control. The agents read, compare, prepare, flag and execute. Never "our AI replaces".
3. **Claims are big and stated flat.** Superlatives stand without hedging ("the leading", "the best"). No hedging words (maybe, can help to, aims to). The proof stands directly next to the claim: a customer's logo, a quote, a number.
4. **Every number has a currency or a percent, and a customer.** "€310,000 in 9 months", "3.4% lower material costs", "75% time saved". A number without an owner is not used.
5. **Industry is named with pride.** Manufacturing, plants, parts, machines, supply chains, Europe, Germany. Procurement is where profit is made, not a back office.
6. **Parallel structure.** Three or four items built the same way: "Read everything. Price every part. Negotiate with proof." Feature lines start with a verb or name the outcome first, then say how: "Price defence: the agent checks each increase against the indices that drive the part."
7. **Before and after.** A story is told as "without us" and "with us", then the proven impact as a short list that starts with the numbers.
8. **Labels are tiny and factual.** Above every headline sits a mono line in capitals that says what this is or who says so: "120 manufacturers buy with Northdesk", "Made in Germany", "Success story", "Powered by ...".

## Words

Use the vocabulary of procurement in manufacturing, exactly and without explaining it:

- People: buyers, strategic purchasing, head of procurement, CPO, suppliers.
- Things: parts, drawing parts, B- and C-parts, articles, categories, material costs, raw materials, indices, order confirmations, RFQs, quotes, framework agreements, volume contracts, tiered prices, lot sizes, should-cost models, cost breakdowns, price increases, savings potential, spend.
- What the AI does: agents analyse, extract, match, compare, flag, alert, prepare, draft, suggest, monitor, escalate only when there is a deviation.
- What the buyer gets: transparency, a price basis, leverage in negotiations, time for strategy, measurable savings, control.
- Brand words the site leans on: industrial AI, frontier AI, AI agents, intelligence (as a product noun), strong, leading, transform, unlock potential.

Words the site never uses (the check warns on them): seamless, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-gen, supercharge, empower, magic, robust, powerful, "we're excited", thrilled, amazing, passionate, 10x.

Not this voice: jokes, puns, questions as headlines, "imagine if", first-person stories about the founders' feelings, apologies, small print about what might not work. (Limits are handled by being specific, not by disclaimers.)

## Punctuation and casing

- Sentence case everywhere. Headlines end with a full stop when they are sentences. Blog titles may be in title case.
- No exclamation marks. None on the whole site.
- No emoji.
- Dashes are spaced: " – " between a claim and its explanation. A colon does the same job inside feature lines.
- Labels are written in normal case and set in mono capitals by the stylesheet. Never type a whole line in capitals.
- Quotes use curly quotation marks and are followed by name, then role and company in mono capitals.
- Product areas are proper nouns with "Intelligence": Supplier Intelligence, Spend & Cost Intelligence. For another company, keep its own product names and do not invent "X Intelligence" names.

## Numbers

- Currency first, compact: €1BN, €38B+, €2M, €700k, €310,000. Percent directly after the figure: 80%, 3.1%. A plus sign for "more than": 120+, 1.4M+.
- One number per card, very large, with a short label above or a two-line mono caption beside it ("of procurement volume saved").
- Always say what it counts and, in stories, over what time: "savings in 8 months".
- Targets make numbers stronger: "3.2% against a target of 2.5%".
- Estimates are marked as estimates in the foot of a document. Never present an invented figure as a customer result.

## Patterns by format

**Landing page.** Chip, two-tone hero, two buttons. Then the product on a panel with a centred head. Then proof on black: customers and four counters. Then the mission as a statement and tall cards. Then one customer story, one impact number with a quote, the agents, industries, more voices, and three giant words to close.

**Success story.** Label "Success story". Title: customer + achievement + how ("Auerberg Guss finds €310,000 in drawing parts"). The headline figure with its time frame. Then four labelled parts: customer description (who they are, in facts: products, sites, employees, export share), without us (what they wanted and what stood in the way), with us (what now happens, which module, what the agents do), proven impact (three to five results, each starting with the number). One quote from a named person.

**Customer POV / one-pager.** Title with the outcome and a time frame for this customer. Their facts in a strip. What we see, what it costs them today (with their own numbers where known, otherwise marked as estimates). Three dated steps. The offer in one sentence. No adjectives about us.

**Deck.** One claim per slide as the headline, written as a sentence. A slide with a number shows only the number, its label and whose it is. Quotes get their own black slide. The last slide is three words and one next step.

**Article.** Opens with two or three very short paragraphs, often one sentence each, that set a scene from industry, not from software. Sub-headings are plain statements or short noun phrases. Paragraphs of one to three sentences. Contrast pairs ("Not because ..., but because ..."). One sentence is pulled out large. It ends with what this means for the reader's plant, not with a sales pitch.

**Post (LinkedIn).** Not measured (posts sit behind a login); derived from the article voice. First line: a flat claim or a customer result with its number. Three to five short lines, each its own paragraph. One named customer or person. Last line: where to read or see more. No hashtag walls, no emoji, no exclamation marks.

**Email.** Subject: the outcome and the customer's name. Three short paragraphs: what we saw at companies like theirs (with one number), what we would do in the first four weeks, one proposed date. Signed with name, role, phone.

**Product UI.** Greeting plus what needs the buyer ("Three things need your approval."). Agent messages as in the table above. Status in two or three words: Signed, Expires soon, Low risk, Needs review. Buttons say what happens: Review and approve, Prepare negotiation.

## German

Read from the German home page. The voice is the same; these are the differences:

- "Einkauf" in running text (20 times against 3 for "Procurement"). The brand terms stay English: Industrial AI, Frontier AI, Supplier Intelligence, Sourcing, Cost Breakdowns. "KI-Agenten", not "AI Agents".
- Feature headlines are infinitive phrases with the object first: "Lieferanten effizient steuern", "Einsparpotenziale sofort realisieren". The hero keeps the short-sentence rhythm: claim, then three fragments.
- Concrete nouns: Artikel, Warengruppen, Lieferanten, Zeichnungsteile, Rahmenverträge, Preiserhöhung, Einsparpotenzial, Vergabeentscheidung.
- Numbers: "700.000 €" and "3,1 %" in running text and quotes; compact in big figures: "€1Mrd", "€2Mio", "€500k", "10 Mio+", "80%".
- Formal address ("Sie"), used rarely: most sentences have no address at all. Spaced en dashes. No exclamation marks.
- Labels: "Im Einsatz bei 120 Herstellern", "Technologieführer", "Gespräch vereinbaren".
- In mono capitals an "ß" becomes "SS" ("GROSSKÜCHENTECHNIK"). That is correct German. If a customer's name contains an ß, make sure the name also appears once in normal case (a title, a lockup, the running text).

Set `<html lang="de">` so numbers count up in the German format and long words hyphenate.
